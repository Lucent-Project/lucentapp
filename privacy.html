PRIVACY

Structural,
not promised

Anyone can promise privacy. Lucent is built so that violating it would require effort — the architecture simply has nowhere for your data to go.

Nothing leaves the device until you export it or give a model a reason to look. That is the entire policy, and the code agrees with it.

01 — ENCRYPTED AT REST

The database wears armour

Your notes live in a SQLCipher-encrypted database; attachments are encrypted individually. The hot paths — PBKDF2 key derivation and AES-256-GCM — run in Rust through JNI, with identical Kotlin fallbacks so the app is never held hostage by a native library. Someone holding your unlocked phone is one thing; someone holding your files gets nothing but noise.

02 — THE APP LOCK

A lock that counts

This is a real brute-force policy, not a polite request: escalating cooldowns, a persisted counter that survives reboots, an optional security question, an optional self-destruct — and fingerprint on Android, Windows Hello on desktop, politely out of office for the whole cooldown. Guess wrong too many times and the app genuinely stops negotiating.

03 — BACKUPS THAT LEAVE WITH YOU

One file. Your pocket. Your password.

Backups are a single .lcb file carrying notes, tasks, history, chats, attachments and settings across devices — previewed before a single item is changed, armed to run automatically, and password-protectable. A backup nobody can read is a keepsake rather than a backup, so this one is read on the way out and again on the way in.

04 — OFF BY DEFAULT

We don't even ask first

Share-sheet integration: off. Diagnostics: off, and kept locally when on. The assistant services you connect are entirely your choice. Cloud mirroring is a module you configure yourself, over WebDAV — your server, your rules. There is no "enabled by default, buried three menus deep" anywhere in this app.

05 — YOUR MODEL, YOUR DATA

The assistant sees only what you show it

Cloud models see exactly what you put in front of them — nothing is synced, indexed or "improved" in the background. Prefer the full hermit mode? Run a .gguf locally and the network can stay off entirely. The one deliberate exception to encryption is exporting to Markdown, Word, PDF or Excel — a file you can open anywhere else, which is rather the point.
